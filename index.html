FILE 01

The Situation

It's an ordinary Tuesday at Ephesus Greeting Card Company, which, for this office, is never ordinary. Two corporate evaluators arrive to decide whether Ephesus deserves recognition as Office of the Year, and discover a floor built on a filing system nobody outside it understands, a copier with feelings, an elevator serviced by a furious man with a Scottish accent, and a coffee station that runs on affirmation as much as caffeine.

A podcast crew shows up to document it all. A father walks in looking for two children he lost at a county fair fifteen years ago. Two kids no one remembers hiring run intelligence operations from the fanciest table in the building. Somewhere between a wellness seminar, a rigged card game, a missing trophy, and a very sincere argument about the color mauve, everyone in the building, evaluators included, has to decide what actually counts as the job.

Inspired by Shakespeare's A Comedy of Errors, Comedy of "Office" Errors asks what happens when mistaken identity, misplaced loyalty, and complete sincerity collide in the last place you'd expect to find any of it: HR.

FILE 06

Behind the Scenes

The people keeping this Tuesday running on schedule, more or less.

Sarah Goss

Director & Playwright

Wrote the whole memo. Now responsible for making twenty-six people believe it. Knows exactly which door sticks, which light cue lies, and which actor will lose their glasses mid-show. Insists she's fine.

Garrett Lee

Assistant Director & Sound

Officially, Assistant Director. Unofficially, the reason the elevator has an accent and the copier has feelings. If it makes a sound tonight, Garrett built it and is somewhere backstage hoping it still works.

Eli Seratt

Assistant Director & Notes

Takes notes on everything, blocking, line reads, who owes whom a snack, and somehow makes all of it sound encouraging. If tonight runs smoothly, it's because Eli wrote it down first.

Chloe Shields

Lighting

Decides what you get to see and when, a lot of power for one person to have over twenty-six actors and one very dramatic elevator. Has never once let the room go dark at the wrong moment. Ask her how close it's been.

McKenna Lanier

Stage Manager

The actual person in charge, regardless of what any job title suggests. Tracks every prop, every cue, and every human in this building, often all three at once, often while several of them hide from her on purpose.

Mike Calnan

Keeper of All Things

Keeps the theatre running, fixes what needs fixing, finds what nobody else can find, and somehow makes the whole place work. Without Mike, we would be lost.
